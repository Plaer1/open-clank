# Choose a proof direction

- For P→Q, the converse is Q→P; the contrapositive is ¬Q→¬P and is equivalent to the original.
- Contradiction instead assumes the negation of the claim; for an implication, assume P and ¬Q, then derive impossibility.
- Claim over all integers n: if n² is even, then n is even. Every integer is even (2k) or odd (2k+1) for some integer k.

**Try:** State P and Q, converse, contrapositive and contradiction assumptions. Choose the contrapositive and prove it algebraically. Explain why that direction is useful and why numerical examples or proving the converse alone would not establish the original claim.
