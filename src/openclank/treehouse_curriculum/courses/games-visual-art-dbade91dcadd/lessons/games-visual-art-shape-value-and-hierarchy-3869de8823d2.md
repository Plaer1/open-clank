# Make the important shape readable

- A silhouette exposes shape before texture. Positive shapes and negative spaces jointly determine what can be distinguished.
- Arrange three values—dark, middle, light—before adding a large palette. Contrast, scale, isolation, edge sharpness and detail can compete for attention.
- Hard, soft and lost edges change separation; repeated shapes create rhythm. Symmetry and asymmetry organize weight differently. These are choices to test, not universal rules of good pictures.

**Self-check:** Draw or describe two small panels using the same three shapes. Change only value grouping or scale to move emphasis from one shape to another; predict and then inspect the result.
