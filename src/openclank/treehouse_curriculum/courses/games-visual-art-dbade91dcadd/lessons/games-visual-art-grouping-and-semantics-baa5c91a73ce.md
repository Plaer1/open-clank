# Grouping changes meaning

- Nearby, similar, connected or jointly moving marks can read as groups. Try conflicting cues: three same-colored dots far apart versus three differently colored dots close together.
- Figure-ground separation asks which region reads as an object. Negative space can make a symbol legible or accidentally join it to a neighbor.
- An icon also relies on convention and context. A triangle may mean play, warning or direction; shape alone does not settle its meaning.
- A style guide records consistent transformations—proportions, edge treatment, palette and symbol meanings—rather than requiring realism.

**Self-check:** Design two states of one button with shape or text as well as color. State the grouping cue and a possible misreading.
