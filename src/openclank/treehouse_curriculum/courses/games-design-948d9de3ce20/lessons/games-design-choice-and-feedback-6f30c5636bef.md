# Choices and perceptible consequences

- A meaningful choice changes an outcome the player can understand. Ten identical buttons add breadth without adding a tradeoff.
- Example: a short exposed route takes 3 turns; a sheltered route takes 5. Risk matters only if exposure can change what happens.
- Separate the mechanic (route lengths), the dynamic (players taking risks), and the intended experience (tension). This is the MDA lens, not proof that players feel what you intended.
- A cue can anticipate an action, acknowledge input and report its result. Show danger with a shape or label as well as color.

**Self-check:** Design two route choices. Name the information a player has before choosing and two different cues after an action.
