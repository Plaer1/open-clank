# Syntax, models, proofs

- Syntax forms expressions; semantics interprets them. A formal proof applies explicit rules.
- From P and P→Q, modus ponens derives Q. A countermodel to an argument satisfies the premises but falsifies its conclusion.
- Soundness says provability preserves semantic consequence; completeness says every semantic consequence in the stated system has a proof.

**Try:** Give a valuation refuting inference P→Q, therefore P. Explain why modus ponens needs both premises.
